Proposed decision procedure:

1. Parse the request into required predicates, optional preferences, alternatives, exclusions, and relationships. Preserve scope: “romance ending tragically” requires that relationship, not separate mentions of love and disaster. Treat ordinary conjunctions as jointly required; explicit preferences affect ordering without becoming requirements. Do not restrict parsing to a fixed genre list.
2. Retrieve broadly, then inspect evidence across each candidate's applicable catalog record. Attach source field, excerpt, edition/mode, and provenance to every predicate. Classify it as supported, contradicted, or unknown; unresolved conflicting sources remain unknown and visibly disputed. Descriptions must entail the claim in context. Authoritative structured fields can establish narrow facts; unverified tags are clues. Similarity, title words, popularity, and repeated labels are not proof. Missing evidence is not evidence of absence.
3. **Supported:** every required predicate is supported, with an evidenced branch for each alternative and no unresolved required conflict. **Partially supported:** at least one required predicate is supported, others are unknown, and none is contradicted. **Unsupported:** a mandatory predicate is contradicted, or none has support. A failed alternative does not invalidate another supported branch. These judgments concern available evidence, not unknowable game content.
4. Negations require affirmative evidence of absence within the requested scope. Silence about combat cannot establish “no combat.” If ambiguity changes eligibility, ask a focused clarification and withhold a definitive recommendation; present conditional judgments for plausible interpretations. Explicitly contradictory requirements require clarification too.
5. Present supported candidates first, then clearly labeled partial alternatives with their precise gaps. Withhold unsupported candidates from recommendations; allow fewer results or none. Never fill a quota with weak matches. Deduplicate excerpts and retain aspect-specific evidence links. Rank within tiers by evidence quality, experience fit, and optional preferences; no title-specific boosts or vetoes.

The accompanying synthetic cases specify evidence judgments and presentation separately. Later implementation should evaluate entire batches, unsupported claims, partial-label accuracy, and retrieval misses separately from catalog gaps. Paraphrasing, reordering clauses, renaming titles, or duplicating tags should preserve judgments. No current catalog was inspected, and no historical bug is asserted to persist.
